# 3. THE ACTUAL PROBLEM

The problem is not simply:

> “People don't recycle e-waste.”

India already has formal recycling infrastructure and regulation.

The problem is the **disconnect between the highly distributed informal collection network and the formal downstream recycling ecosystem**.

The official PS identifies the informal ecosystem's reach and low collection cost as important reasons why informal collectors remain deeply involved in end-of-life electronics. It then identifies informational and institutional gaps around prices, registered recyclers, compliant handover, and documented transactions. ([SIH 2026 Problem Statements][1])

A recent 2026 study interviewing 23 informal e-waste collectors in Delhi found three broad classes of barriers: government/policy awareness, difficulties dealing with formal recyclers including awareness of registered recyclers and competitive pricing, and constraints within the informal sector itself. ([ScienceDirect][2])

That strongly validates the product direction.

# 5. WHY THE MINISTRY OF MINES CARES

The project has a very strong policy connection because the Ministry of Mines is actively supporting critical-mineral recycling.

The Union Cabinet approved a **₹1,500 crore Critical Mineral Recycling Incentive Scheme** in September 2025 covering eligible feedstocks including e-waste and spent lithium-ion battery scrap. The scheme is intended to increase domestic recycling capacity and extraction of critical minerals from secondary sources. ([Ministry of Mines][3])

Therefore the long-term chain can be framed as:

```text
Informal Collection
        ↓
Formal Channelisation
        ↓
Responsible Recycling
        ↓
Secondary Material Recovery
        ↓
Potential Critical-Mineral Recovery
        ↓
Supply-Chain Resilience
```

But do not say:

> “Every collected PCB generates X grams of gold.”

That requires material-specific processing data.

Use:

> **“The platform supports better channelisation of secondary resources and potential critical-mineral recovery through formal recycling.”**

# 6. VERY IMPORTANT — REGULATORY MODEL

This is one of the areas where the previous AI reports occasionally overreached.

## Our product is NOT the CPCB portal.

CPCB operates the official E-Waste Management System under the E-Waste (Management) Rules, 2022, including EPR registration, registered stakeholders, recycler SOPs, EPR certificates and related workflows. ([EPR Waste][4])

We build an **independent operational layer**.

# 8. BATTERIES NEED THEIR OWN ROUTE

The official SIH problem mentions batteries as an example material. ([SIH 2026 Problem Statements][1])

However, CPCB's E-Waste FAQ specifically excludes waste batteries covered by the Battery Waste Management Rules, 2022 from the E-Waste Management Rules. CPCB maintains a separate Battery EPR Portal. ([EPR Waste][5])

So the architecture must distinguish:

```text
ROUTE A
E-Waste
   ↓
E-Waste registered recycler
```

and

```text
ROUTE B
Battery Waste
   ↓
Battery-regulation registered recycler/refurbisher
```

and potentially:

```text

Other recyclable material
   ↓
Applicable waste-stream pathway
```

This is an excellent judge-Q&A point.

# 109. UNIT ECONOMICS FORMULA

### Existing net

```text
Existing Net =
Sale Value
− Travel Cost
− Other Selling Costs
```

### Platform net

```text
Platform Net =
Final Sale Value
− Travel / Pickup Cost
− Platform Cost
```

### Improvement

```text
Net Improvement =
Platform Net − Existing Net
```

Then:

```text
Improvement %
=
(Net Improvement / Existing Net) × 100
```

Only present actual percentages when the underlying field data supports them.

# 126. QUESTION — “WHAT IF AI IS WRONG?”

Answer:

> “AI is advisory. The user confirms the category, and low-confidence results fall back to manual selection. The final agreed material and price are recorded at handover.”

---

# 127. QUESTION — “HOW DO YOU KNOW THE RECYCLER IS AUTHORIZED?”

Answer:

> “We don't infer authorization from an AI model. We maintain a source-backed recycler registry with the relevant registration/authorization reference, source and verification date. Records without adequate verification aren't presented as verified destinations.”

---

# 128. QUESTION — “WHAT IF THE PRICE IS WRONG?”

Answer:

> “We don't present one arbitrary number as the market price. We provide an indicative local range based on dated observations and recycler quotes, together with confidence and source information.”

---

# 129. QUESTION — “WHY WOULD A COLLECTOR USE IT?”

Answer:

> “Because it reduces three practical frictions: knowing the indicative value of material, finding an appropriate downstream destination, and proving the transaction. The collector doesn't need to abandon cash or completely change their existing business.”

---

# 130. QUESTION — “WHAT IF THEY DON'T HAVE A SMARTPHONE?”

Answer:

> “The long-term architecture supports an assisted-agent mode where an aggregator, SHG member or field worker can operate the platform on behalf of collectors. We would not force a smartphone-only assumption onto the entire informal ecosystem.”

---

# 131. QUESTION — “IS THIS JUST ANOTHER SCRAP MARKETPLACE?”

Answer:

> “No. A marketplace is only one component. Our core unit is the traceable material lot: source, classification, price observation, destination, handover evidence and downstream confirmation, designed for the informal collector and low-connectivity field environment.”

---

# 132. QUESTION — “IS YOUR QR AN EPR CERTIFICATE?”

Answer:

> “No. It is a digital handover and traceability record. Official EPR certificates remain within the CPCB regulatory workflow.”

---

# 133. QUESTION — “WHAT ABOUT BATTERIES?”

Answer:

> “Battery waste has a separate regulatory route under the Battery Waste Management Rules, so our system models batteries as a separate compliance pathway rather than incorrectly treating them all as E-Waste Rules transactions.” ([EPR Waste][5])

---

# 134. QUESTION — “WHERE IS YOUR DATA FROM?”

Answer:

> “We separate data provenance into official sources, state/regulatory sources, field research, recycler observations, platform-generated transactions and synthetic demonstration data. Each record carries its source classification.”

---

# 135. QUESTION — “WHY NOT USE REAL-TIME PRICES?”

Answer:

> “Real-time price feeds are not necessary for proving the core product. We provide dated indicative observations with confidence and can add more automated feeds later. This also prevents a third-party API failure from breaking the collector workflow.”

---

# 136. QUESTION — “WHAT HAPPENS OFFLINE?”

Answer:

> “The core transaction is created locally and enters an outbox. Once connectivity is restored, the system validates and synchronizes the operation using an idempotent operation ID.”

---

# 137. QUESTION — “CAN THIS SCALE?”

Answer:

> “The application separates configuration from code. Materials, regulatory routes, recycler coverage, price observations and language packs are data-driven. So the pilot can expand region by region without rewriting the core architecture.”

---

# 138. QUESTION — “WHY NOT USE WHATSAPP?”

Answer:

> “WhatsApp can support communication, but our product creates structured lots, source-aware pricing, destination matching, offline records, transaction states and standardized handover evidence as one workflow.”

---

# 139. QUESTION — “WHY SHOULD RECYCLERS PARTICIPATE?”

Answer:

> “We give them structured incoming material information, a consistent lot workflow and better traceability. The platform can eventually reduce acquisition friction and improve their visibility into informal collection streams.”

Do not promise guaranteed supply.

6. Ground-truth corrections — apply these before you design anything
These came out of cross-checking all seven rounds against the actual regulatory text. Getting any of these wrong in front of a judge who knows the EPR system is an easy, avoidable loss of credibility.

Your handover record is NOT an "EPR Certificate." EPR certificates are issued by CPCB, in favor of registered recyclers, through the official EPR Portal. Call your artifact a "Traceable Handover Record" or "Digital Chain-of-Custody Record." You can honestly say a registered recycler could use compatible records as supporting evidence in their own compliance workflow — but you are not issuing the certificate.
Never label a collector "CPCB-authorized." Only formally registered recyclers/dismantlers/refurbishers/producers hold that status. Use your own application-level identity instead: e.g. "Collector ID" (an app identity, not a government credential).
Batteries are a separate regulatory track. The Battery Waste Management Rules, 2022 govern batteries outside the E-Waste Rules, with their own CPCB Battery EPR Portal. Route batteries to a distinct "Battery Waste" regulatory path in your data model rather than lumping them under generic e-waste.
Recycler verification needs levels, not a binary badge. Don't display "Verified Recycler" for anything self-declared. Use a tiered scheme (e.g., Level 4 = CPCB/SPCB list-verified → Level 0 = demo/synthetic) and only surface Level 3+ as "Verified Formal Destination" to collectors.
Don't invent a ministry formula. No published "JNARDDC stoichiometric formula" exists for mineral-content estimation from photos (§4, §9.2). Use citable, published e-waste-composition research instead, labeled explicitly as a secondary-source estimate.
Don't overstate the informal-sector share as an official statistic. See §7 — the government has stated in Parliament that it does not have a national estimate of the informal sector's share of e-waste. Widely-quoted "~90–95% informal" or "<1% formally recycled" figures are industry/press estimates, not CPCB numbers — and they're increasingly out of date (see below).

9.2 The four AI/ML features (build in this priority order)
The PS says AI/ML is required "wherever sufficient training data is available" — that phrase is your license to be honest about where it isn't, and a hybrid (AI-suggests, human-confirms) design is more defensible than a fully automated one.

Material classification — fine-tune a small pretrained CNN (MobileNetV2/V3) via transfer learning; export to TensorFlow Lite / TF.js so it runs on-device, fully offline. Train free via Google Colab/Kaggle GPU or no-code via Google Teachable Machine. Target 8–10 classes matching the PS's named categories, plus a mandatory UNKNOWN/OTHER class so the model can say "not confident" instead of forcing a wrong label. Always keep a manual icon-picker fallback — never make AI classification mandatory for the flow to proceed.
Valuation estimate — "two numbers, not one," done honestly:
Cash price = current local rate (₹/kg, from your Price Dataset) × condition/grade multiplier.
Recovery Score (0–100), not a cash value = a relative indicator built from published, citable e-waste-composition research (e.g., studies reporting PCB metal content in the range of roughly 10–40% by weight, gold content from the tens up to a few hundred grams per tonne depending on device type, and copper content in double-digit kilograms per tonne for typical device PCBs — cite the actual papers you use). Label it explicitly: "Estimate based on published secondary-source composition data, not a certified lab assay." This gives you the same "two numbers" demo beat the invented JNARDDC formula was chasing, without an unverifiable authority.
Recycler matching/ranking — a transparent weighted score, not a black box: material accepted (hard filter) → authorization status (hard filter — never rank an unauthorized destination above an authorized one) → offered rate → distance → pickup availability → historical reliability. Show the "why recommended" reasoning on the card.
Anomaly/abnormal-value detection — start rule-based and explainable: flag if final_price < 0.70 × rolling local median for that category/location, or if final weight deviates >30–35% from the estimate, or if the same image hash is reused across active lots. Upgrade to Isolation Forest once you have real transaction volume; always keep a human-review step — a judge will trust an explainable threshold far more than an opaque "AI flagged it."
The 20/80 rule for team time: AI/codegen can quickly produce boilerplate (CRUD, UI scaffolding, basic sync code). It cannot decide, and you must own: the offline conflict-resolution policy, the incentive design (why does a collector actually switch?), the handover-record schema/semantics, and the unit-economics numbers.

9.3 Offline-first design (the part a judge will ask you to trace step by step)
Collector opens the installed PWA with zero signal → Service Worker serves the cached app shell instantly.
Lot creation: photo → on-device classification (works offline) → weight → value estimate computed from the last-synced local copy of the Price Dataset → written to IndexedDB as pending.
Handover: photo + GPS + timestamp + a locally generated hash and UUID → queued locally, whether or not connectivity exists.
On reconnect: Background Sync (or a manual retry loop, since iOS Safari's background-sync support is weaker) drains the queue; Price and Recycler datasets re-sync down for the next offline session.
Recycler confirms from their console (online) → traceability record updates → collector's earnings ledger updates on next sync.
Conflict rules (decide and document before writing UI code): two devices confirming the same handover → first-confirmed wins, second is auto-flagged "disputed" for recycler review. Price changes between capture and sync → server flags a price_dispute, collector sees both values and taps to accept the one actually agreed at handover. Recycler-entered final weight supersedes the collector's estimate, with the original retained in an audit trail. Everything gets a locally generated UUID/idempotency key so retries never create duplicates.

Demo insurance: rehearse turning on airplane mode on stage, completing a full lot-to-handover flow, then reconnecting and showing the sync happen live. This single beat outperforms any slide. Always have a pre-recorded fallback video and a localhost/Docker Compose version in case venue Wi-Fi fails.

14. Field research (do this before writing UI code)
The PS requires interviews with at least two real, working scrap collectors or aggregators — this is checkable and most teams will skip or fake it.

How, practically: most Indian cities have informal scrap clusters (e.g., in Delhi: Mundka, Mayapuri, Seelampur for various categories). A short, respectful visit — buy something small, or simply explain you're students researching for a government hackathon — is usually enough for 15–20 minutes of conversation.

Question script:

What kinds of e-waste do you currently collect/buy, and roughly what do you pay/charge per kg for the 2–3 most common items?
How do you currently decide what price to offer or accept?
Where does this material go after you — do you know if it reaches an authorized recycler?
What do you do with circuit boards, batteries, and CRTs specifically — dismantle, burn, acid-treat?
Would you use a phone app that showed fair prices and the nearest authorized buyer? What would stop you (literacy, phone type, trust, time)?
Roughly how much do you earn in a typical week?
Deliverables from this visit (do all of these): photos (with consent), a short write-up, real per-kg price numbers for your Price Dataset, ideally a screen recording of the collector actually trying your prototype's core flow — this single visit becomes your live-usability evidence, your Price Dataset seed, and your unit-economics source data.

# Master blueprint: SIH26229

I read the complete attached consolidation and cross-checked its central claims against the official SIH26229 brief, CPCB material, the E-Waste Management Rules, the Ministry of Mines’ critical-mineral policy documents, and current competitor information. The strongest interpretation is that SIH26229 is **not merely a scrap marketplace or AI classification app**; it is an offline-first trust, transaction and traceability layer connecting independent informal collectors with the formal recycling ecosystem.

The following should become your project’s source of truth.

***

## 1. Problem statement

### Official identity

| Item | Details |
|---|---|
| Problem ID | SIH26229 |
| Title | Kabadiwala Connect – Bringing the Informal Collector into the Formal Recycling Chain |
| Category | Software |
| Theme | Clean & Green Technology |
| Organization | Ministry of Mines |
| Department | JNARDDC |
| Deadline | 30 September 2026 |

The official brief asks for a vernacular, low-literacy, offline-tolerant mobile platform that enables informal scrap collectors to discover fair prices, connect with authorized recyclers, complete a documented handover and receive payment. It also explicitly requires a recycler-side interface, structured datasets, field research with at least two working collectors or aggregators, a live usability demonstration and a unit-economics assessment. [sih2026.vuce](https://sih2026.vuce.in/ps/SIH26229)

### The actual problem

Informal collectors are already the strongest last-mile collection network, but they often lack:

- Reliable price information.
- Access to verified formal recyclers.
- Knowledge of which materials a recycler accepts.
- Safe handling guidance.
- A structured transaction history.
- Proof of handover.
- A mechanism to compare buyer offers.
- Connectivity-independent tools.

This creates a gap between collection and formal recycling. Materials may be burned, acid-leached, manually dismantled or sold through opaque channels. The result is lost material value, unsafe working conditions, environmental damage and little incentive for collectors to use formal channels. [sih2026.vuce](https://sih2026.vuce.in/ps/SIH26229)

### The product thesis

> **Make formal recycling easier, safer, more transparent and economically attractive for the informal collector than the current route.**

This is the central product question. Everything else—AI, price discovery, QR codes, maps and analytics—must support it.

## 3. Regulatory and domain rules

### EPR certificate boundary

The application should create a:

> **Digital Handover Record / Chain-of-Custody Record**

It should not claim to generate an official EPR certificate.

Under the E-Waste Management Rules, 2022, EPR certificates are generated through the CPCB portal in favour of registered recyclers. The rules also state that manufacturers, producers, refurbishers and recyclers must register in the relevant categories. [moef.gov](https://moef.gov.in/uploads/2022/11/E-Waste-Management-Rules-2022.pdf)

Use this wording in the product:

> “This is a verifiable platform-generated handover record. It is not an official CPCB EPR certificate.”

### Collector identity

Do not call a collector:

- CPCB-authorized recycler.
- Government-verified collector.
- Certified recycler.

Use:

> **ReLoop Collector ID**

This is an application identity, not a government license.

### Recycler verification levels

Use explicit provenance:

| Level | Meaning | Collector-facing label |
|---|---|---|
| Level 4 | Confirmed through current official registry | Official-source verified |
| Level 3 | State PCB/PCC source confirmed | State-source verified |
| Level 2 | Partner documents or direct verification | Document verified |
| Level 1 | Self-declared facility | Self-declared |
| Level 0 | Demonstration or synthetic record | Demo data |

Only Levels 3 and 4 should receive a strong “verified formal destination” presentation.

Every recycler record needs:

```text
recycler_id
name
facility_address
latitude
longitude
materials_accepted
regulatory_route
registration_reference
verification_level
verification_source
source_url
source_date
last_verified_at
offered_rates
pickup_available
service_area
```

CPCB maintains an official e-waste recycler/dismantler information page, which should be treated as the primary source for the initial recycler registry. [cpcb.nic](https://cpcb.nic.in/e-waste-recyclers-dismantler/)

### Batteries are a separate route

The E-Waste Management Rules, 2022 exclude waste batteries covered by the Battery Waste Management Rules, 2022. [moef.gov](https://moef.gov.in/uploads/2022/11/E-Waste-Management-Rules-2022.pdf)

Therefore do not route every battery through an ordinary e-waste recycler.

Create separate regulatory routes:

```text
E-WASTE ROUTE
PCB, LCD, CRT, cable, electronic assembly, motor

BATTERY ROUTE
Li-ion, lead-acid and covered battery waste

OTHER MATERIAL ROUTE
Mixed plastics and other applicable waste streams
```

The application should show the route transparently:

> “Battery material: route to a battery-regulation recycler.”

### Ministry of Mines connection

The Ministry of Mines’ ₹1,500 crore Critical Mineral Recycling Incentive Scheme includes e-waste, spent lithium-ion batteries and other eligible scrap as feedstock. The scheme’s actual objective is to incentivize recycling capacity and extraction of critical minerals, not simply collection. [mines.gov](https://mines.gov.in/admin/storage/ckeditor/21__Replies_to_Parliament_Questions_in_Rajya_Sabha_1780919767.pdf)

Therefore the correct strategic narrative is:

```text
Informal collection
   ↓
Formal routing
   ↓
Safe recycling
   ↓
Secondary material recovery
   ↓
Critical-mineral supply resilience
```

Do not claim that every collected kilogram produces a fixed amount of lithium, cobalt, copper, gold or rare earths. Recovery depends on material composition and the recycler’s process.

### Module 3: Material taxonomy


Always include **Unknown / Other**. AI must be allowed to say that it is not confident.

### Module 4: Price board

The price board must not show a single authoritative number.

Example:

```text
Printed Circuit Board

Indicative range:
₹X–₹Y per kg

Latest observation:
27 September 2026

Observations:
18

Confidence:
Medium

Sources:
Field observations + recycler quotes
```

Required fields include category, subcategory, location, date/time, buying price, quoted price, unit, historical range and recycler/aggregator offer. [sih2026.vuce](https://sih2026.vuce.in/ps/SIH26229)

### Price methodology

For the MVP:

1. Remove invalid observations.
2. Filter by category and subcategory.
3. Filter or weight by geography.
4. Weight recent observations more heavily.
5. Use weighted median rather than a simple average.
6. Generate an indicative range.
7. Calculate confidence.
8. Display the latest observation date.

Confidence levels:

```text
HIGH
Many recent verified local observations.

MEDIUM
Several observations but limited coverage.

LOW
Few observations or older data.

INSUFFICIENT
No credible local data.
```

A displayed price should always carry:

```text
source_type
source_name
observed_at
location
confidence
```

### Module 5: Recycler matching

Matching should be deterministic and explainable before introducing machine learning.

Filter by:

1. Material accepted.
2. Regulatory route.
3. Verification level.
4. Service area.
5. Distance.
6. Offered rate.
7. Pickup availability.
8. Recent reliability.

Example ranking:

```text
Material compatibility: 35%
Authorization/verification: 25%
Offered rate: 20%
Distance: 10%
Pickup availability: 5%
Reliability: 5%
```

Do not describe this as machine learning unless a trained model is actually used.

Use:

> **Constraint-aware recycler matching**

## 8. AI and analytics plan

### Feature priority

| Feature | MVP approach | Advanced approach |
|---|---|---|
| Material classification | Lightweight image classifier | On-device model with continual improvement |
| Price estimation | Weighted median and range | XGBoost or time-series model |
| Quote anomaly | IQR or MAD | Anomaly model |
| Recycler matching | Rule-based ranking | Learning-to-rank |
| Weight inconsistency | Threshold rules | Risk-scoring model |
| Voice entry | Optional API | On-device Indian-language ASR |

### Price anomaly detection

For each material and region:

```text
median_price
Q1
Q3
IQR = Q3 - Q1
```

Flag a quote if it is far outside the local historical range.

Use cautious language:

> “This quote differs significantly from recent observations.”

Do not say:

> “The recycler is cheating you.”

### Traceability anomaly

Example:

```text
Collected weight: 10.0 kg
Received weight: 7.1 kg
Variance: 29%
```

Show:

> Review required: weight variance detected.

Do not automatically accuse either party.

### Model evaluation

Measure:

- Accuracy.
- Precision.
- Recall.
- Macro-F1.
- Per-class F1.
- Confusion matrix.
- Manual-correction rate.
- Low-confidence rate.

With a small or imbalanced dataset, macro-F1 is more informative than raw accuracy.

### Dataset safeguards

Avoid leakage. Multiple photographs of the same physical object should remain in the same split.

Recommended initial split:

```text
70% training
15% validation
15% testing
```

The test set must represent field conditions:

- Dirty objects.
- Cluttered backgrounds.
- Low light.
- Damaged materials.
- Mixed components.
- Different camera qualities.

- Phone OTP or demo credentials.
- Short session expiry for privileged roles.
- Role-based access control.
- Server-side authorization checks.
- No privileged secrets in the frontend.

- Foreign keys.
- Row-level security where applicable.
- Unique constraints.
- Check constraints.
- Audit logs.
- Rate limiting.
- Input validation.

- Compress before upload.
- Strip EXIF metadata.

- Capture only when needed.

- No unnecessary contacts.

## 3. Product definition (final)

**ReLoop Setu** — *Fair Price. Verified Recycler. Traceable Handover.*

One-liner: **A vernacular, offline-first trust and traceability layer that turns informal e-waste collection into a structured, price-transparent route to verified formal recycling.**

The five pillars:

1. **FairRate** — source-aware indicative price ranges with confidence, never fake "real-time market price."
2. **RecycleMatch** — regulatory-route-aware matching to verified destinations (E-Waste route vs Battery route vs Other).
3. **Lot Passport** — the lot lifecycle: CREATED → COLLECTED → LISTED → MATCHED → ACCEPTED → HANDED_OVER → RECEIVED → CLOSED (+ REJECTED/CANCELLED/EXPIRED). Backend validates every transition; illegal transitions rejected.
4. **EarnLedger** — transparent earnings/payment history; builds the collector's only-ever digital income record.
5. **Offline Setu** — the full core workflow without internet. *"The internet can disappear. The transaction cannot."*

AI sits across these as an assistant (Classify → Estimate → Flag → Recommend), never as the source of truth.

## 4. Regulatory rules — non-negotiable design constraints

These are where naive teams get destroyed in Q&A:

1. **Your receipt is NOT an EPR certificate.** Call it *Digital Handover Record / Chain-of-Custody Record*. EPR certificates are generated through CPCB's E-Waste EPR portal in favour of registered recyclers. Say: "Our export adapter produces evidence a recycler may use in its compliance workflow; official certificates remain with CPCB."
2. **Never badge a collector as "CPCB-authorized."** Only manufacturers, producers, refurbishers and recyclers register on the EPR portal. Collectors get a **ReLoop Collector ID** — an application identity, nothing more.
3. **Batteries are a separate regulatory track.** Waste batteries fall under the Battery Waste Management Rules, 2022 (separate CPCB Battery EPR portal), outside the E-Waste Rules. Architecture: Route A (E-Waste → e-waste-registered recycler), Route B (Battery → battery-registered recycler/refurbisher), Route C (other recyclables). This one answer beats most competitors.
4. **Recycler verification levels:** L4 official registry verified / L3 state-PCB source verified / L2 partner-document verified / L1 self-declared / L0 demo-synthetic. Only L3+ display as "Verified Formal Destination." Every record carries `source, source_url, retrieved_at, last_verified_at`.
5. **Data minimization:** no Aadhaar, no PAN, no bank details, no exact home address, no continuous location. Phone (optional) + alias + language + general operating area. Collector dataset excludes unnecessary PII — and say that out loud as a design decision (DPDP Rules 2025 phased commencement makes this prudent).

## 8. Offline-first architecture (the 80% you must own)

Flow: app shell served from Service Worker → lot created offline (photo compressed <150KB WebP, EXIF stripped; TF.js classification on-device; price from last-synced cached Price Dataset) → written to IndexedDB + sync_outbox (`operation_id, entity, entity_id, operation, payload, created_at, retry_count, offline_uuid` for idempotency) → handover record hashed locally (SHA-256 over `{transaction_id, receipt_hash, issued_at}`) → on reconnect, queue drains to FastAPI → validation → Postgres → ACK → marked synced.

**Conflict policy (decide now, cite in Q&A):**
- Reference data (prices, recyclers): server-wins / last-write-wins.
- Transactions: never silently overwrite. Version + event log + state-transition validation (`ACCEPTED → HANDED_OVER` valid; `CLOSED → CREATED` rejected).
- Two devices claim same handover → first-confirmed wins, second auto-flagged "disputed" for recycler review.
- Price changed between capture and sync → flag `price_dispute`, show both, collector taps to accept; agreed price recorded as `final_value`.
- Recycler's measured weight supersedes collector estimate, audit trail retained; >35% variance → "Review Required" flag (never an accusation).

**Offline Queue Monitor UI (judge-visible):** "Offline mode · 3 records saved locally · ✓ Lot created · ✓ Handover recorded · ⏳ Sync pending · Last sync 3:42 PM."

**Acceptance test that must pass before demo:** offline lot creation → cached price board + recycler directory accessible → handover record generated → reconnect → syncs exactly once (no duplicates) → recycler sees lot → collector earnings update.

## 10. AI/ML plan (assistive, honest, defensible)

1. **Classification:** transfer-learned MobileNetV3-Small (alt EfficientNet-B0), quantized, on-device. Output = suggestion + confidence + [Confirm]/[Change]. Below threshold → "AI confidence low — please select manually." Mandatory UNKNOWN/OTHER class. Metrics: Macro-F1 (not raw accuracy), per-class F1, confusion matrix, manual-correction rate. Declared fallback: icon picker. Never let AI block a transaction.
2. **Valuation:** statistical first — **recency-weighted median** (not mean; scrap prices have outliers) per material+geography → range + confidence (HIGH/MEDIUM/LOW/INSUFFICIENT) + observation count + date. Display always as *indicative range* with "final price depends on grade, condition, weight, recycler inspection."
3. **Recovery Score (the CMRIS hook):** cite published e-waste composition literature (PCB metal content ~10–40% by weight; Au tens-to-hundreds g/tonne; Cu ~130 kg/t in mobile PCBs — cite the actual papers) → a **relative 0–100 Recovery Score + estimate label**, clearly "estimate from published secondary composition data, not a lab assay." Never a fake "JNARDDC stoichiometric formula" — a competing team's repo claims one; it doesn't exist, and a judge from JNARDDC would end them.
4. **Anomaly flags:** MAD or IQR band on price; weight-variance rule; duplicate-image hash; "quote significantly below recent local observations — review before accepting" (never "recycler is cheating").
5. **Matching:** deterministic weighted score (material acceptance hard filter incl. regulatory route → authorization status → distance 30% / price 30% / pickup 20% / service area 15% / reliability 5%). Call it "constraint-aware matching," not ML. Learning-to-rank is Phase 4+.

20/80 split: AI builds CRUD, pipelines, charts, tests; humans own regulatory logic, taxonomy, verification, pricing method, sync/conflict policy, incentive design, safety rules, data provenance.

## 6️⃣ The AI/ML Layer — Enough Detail to Defend It Live

### 6.1 Material Classification
Fine-tune **MobileNetV3-Small** on the combined Roboflow + TrashBox + self-collected images. Export to quantized TFLite. Run **client-side** so it works fully offline. Target 7–8 classes: PCB, Cable, LCD, CRT, Battery, Motor, Mixed Plastic, Unknown. **Include a mandatory "Unknown / Unable to confidently identify" class** — this is critical for AI safety.

### 6.2 Valuation Estimation — Grounded in Real Research
**Do not** claim a nonexistent "JNARDDC formula." Instead, build a transparent two-part estimator:
- **Base weight-based rate:** current scrap-category price from the Price Dataset (₹/kg, field-sourced)
- **Critical-mineral recovery indicator (a "Recovery Score," not a cash value):** Use **published PCB composition benchmarks**. Peer-reviewed sources consistently report waste PCBs containing roughly **10–40% metal by weight**, with **gold content in the tens to low-hundreds of grams per tonne** and **copper content around 130 kg per tonne of mobile-phone PCBs**. Use these published ranges — cited to the actual papers — to compute a **relative Recovery Score (0–100)**. Label it explicitly as an **estimate based on published secondary-source composition data, not a lab assay**.

### 6.3 Anomaly Detection
Simple, explainable: flag any quoted or final price that deviates more than ~2 standard deviations from the rolling historical price for that category/location. Use Median Absolute Deviation or IQR. **Do not over-engineer into a black-box model.**

### 6.4 Recycler Matching
Weighted-score ranking: material acceptance (30%), authorization status (25%), distance (15%), offered rate (20%), pickup availability (5%), reliability (5%). Transparent, tunable, explainable. **Never rank an unauthorized recycler above an authorized one.**

## 7️⃣ Offline-First Architecture — The Flow a Judge Will Ask You to Trace

1. Collector opens installed PWA with no signal → Service Worker serves cached app shell instantly.
2. They capture a lot: photo → client-side TFLite classification (works offline) → weight entry → value estimate from **last-synced local Price Dataset** (cached in IndexedDB) → written to IndexedDB as pending record.
3. Handover happens (with or without signal): photo + GPS + timestamp + SHA-256 hash generated **locally**, queued in IndexedDB.
4. When connectivity returns: Background Sync pushes queued records to Supabase; Price and Recycler datasets re-sync down for next offline session.
5. Recycler confirms handover (online) → traceability record updates → collector's earnings ledger updates on next sync.

**Demo this literally:** turn on airplane mode on stage, complete a full lot-to-handover flow, then reconnect and show the sync happen. This single demo beat is worth more than any slide.

📜 Part 2 — Regulatory Grounding (the layer most teams will get wrong)

2.1 Roles under the E-Waste (Management) Rules 2022

Only manufacturers, producers, refurbishers and recyclers must register on the CPCB EPR portal (dismantlers register separately with SPCBs). Therefore your system distinguishes four role tiers — Collector → Aggregator → Registered Recycler → Producer/PRO — and never displays a fake "Government Verified Collector" badge. Use your own "ReLoop Collector ID."

2.2 The receipt vs. the certificate

Your QR artifact is a "Traceable Handover Record" with a SHA-256 hash (optionally Ed25519-signed). A registered recycler may later use compatible transaction records inside its CPCB compliance workflow, but official EPR certificates are generated only within CPCB's system. Say this before a judge asks.

🏗️ Part 4 — Product Definition

The five pillars





FairRate — source-aware indicative pricing (weighted median + range + confidence, never a bare average; outlier-robust).



RecycleMatch — verified destination matching (constraint filters → weighted ranking: distance 30%, offered price 30%, pickup 20%, service area 15%, reliability 5%; hard rule: unauthorized recyclers never rank above authorized). Deterministic and explainable — do not call it ML.



Lot Passport — traceable material lifecycle: DRAFT → COLLECTED → LISTED → MATCHED → ACCEPTED → HANDED_OVER → RECEIVED → CLOSED (+ REJECTED/CANCELLED), backend-validated state machine, append-only audit log.



EarnLedger — transactions, payment status (PENDING/PARTIAL/PAID), monthly totals; records payment, never executes it (cash-first; UPI optional, never mandatory).



Offline Setu — the core workflow (create lot, price check from cache, recycler directory, handover record, receipt) works with zero connectivity.

AI sits across the modules as an assistant — Classify → Estimate → Detect anomalies → Recommend — never as the source of transaction truth.

Personas & jobs-to-be-done





Collector (primary): "Tell me what this is worth, who can buy it safely, where to take it, and prove the transaction." Low-literacy, entry-level Android, intermittent connectivity, price-sensitive.



Aggregator: multi-collector lots, inventory, batch creation, price comparison.



Registered recycler: incoming discovery, accept/quote/receive, confirm handover, transaction history, EPR-manifest export.



Admin/program: verification records, flows, trends, data-quality, impact metrics.

Design principle: ONE ACTION → ONE DECISION → ONE OUTCOME

The collector flow is four questions: 1. What did you collect? (picture cards) · 2. How much? (big numeric) · 3. Photo. · 4. Continue. Advanced fields hide behind "More details." This is genuine low-literacy design, not a translated English dashboard. Every primary button is large, every label short; audio (pre-generated Hindi/Marathi clips for critical prompts — price, safety, handover success) so runtime TTS is never a hard dependency.

Hero screens

Create Lot (big camera button — starts the whole chain) · Price Board (range, trend sparkline, observation count, confidence, last-updated) · Lot Passport (checklist of completed lifecycle stages).

Sync & conflict policy (the 80% you must own — decide before coding)





Local write always wins immediately — collector never waits on the network; UI shows "साठवले ✅".



Outbox entries carry client-generated UUIDs → idempotency keys (offline_uuid); server bulk-upsert is idempotent — no duplicate transactions on retry.



Reference data (prices, directory): server-wins. Transactions: never silently overwritten — versioned event log + legal-state-transition validation (ACCEPTED → HANDED_OVER valid; CLOSED → CREATED rejected server-side).



Conflicts: dual claims on one handover → first-confirmed wins, second flagged "Review Required" (not "fraud"); recycler's confirmed weight/grade supersedes estimate, with collector estimate retained in the audit trail. Weight variance >15–20% raises a review flag automatically.



Offline indicator + queue monitor screen ("3 records saved locally · last sync 3:42 PM") — make offline operation visible to judges.

#



Dataset



Build plan





1



Material



Curated reference table (~40–60 rows: CRT, LCD, PCB, cable types, battery chemistries, motors/magnets, mixed plastics) from published composition literature + UNKNOWN/OTHER class (AI safety: the system may say "unable to confidently identify"); grows as real lots are logged

4



Transaction

5



Traceability



Hash-chained handover records from the QR flow, updated on recycler confirmation





6



Collector

7



AI/ML training



Roboflow Universe CC-BY e-waste sets (~257-image classification set, PCB component set, e-waste detection set) + TrashBox (e-waste class, 2,883 imgs) + TrashNet + 300–500 self-captured photos from your scrap-market visit (30–50/class, varied angles/backgrounds). Every image carries source/license/date metadata

Classifier pipeline (fast, honest, offline)





Self-collect photos (field visit) → augment (rotate/flip/crop/brightness via albumentations) → ~600–800/class effective.



Fine-tune MobileNetV3-Small / EfficientNet-B0 in free Colab/Kaggle (or Teachable Machine → export quantized TFLite, 1–2 MB).



Split 70/15/15 grouped by physical object (no leakage: same PCB's photos never span train/test).



Evaluate with Macro-F1, per-class F1, confusion matrix (macro-F1 over accuracy for a small imbalanced set); test set must include dirty/low-light/cluttered field conditions.



Human-in-the-loop: AI suggests + confidence; user confirms/changes; below threshold → "please select manually." Declared fallback: weak class → icon-picker. Judges respect a declared fallback over an overclaim.



Runs client-side (TF.js) → classification works in airplane mode.

Valuation: two numbers, both honest





Cash estimate: field-sourced ₹/kg from the Price Dataset (never hardcoded).



Recovery Score (0–100) + CO₂-offset estimate: from published PCB composition research — waste PCBs ~10–40% metal by weight; gold ~80–490 g/tonne depending on device class; copper ~130 kg/tonne of mobile-phone PCBs in commonly cited studies — labeled "estimate from published secondary-source composition data, not a lab assay." Never cite the nonexistent "JNARDDC stoichiometric formula" some competing teams use.

Anomaly detection (explainable, not black-box)

Flag quotes >~2 SD (or IQR band / rolling MAD) from category-location history: "⚠ This price is below recent observed local prices — review before accepting." Not "recycler is cheating." Later: weight-variance flags, duplicate-photo/lot detection, repeated-transaction patterns → all labeled Risk Flags, never "fraud."

Provenance & synthetic labeling (your data moat)

Every record: source_type (OFFICIAL | STATE_PCB | FIELD_RESEARCH | RECYCLER_QUOTE | PUBLIC_MARKET | SYNTHETIC), source_name, source_url, captured_at, verified_at, confidence, data_version. Admin data-quality screen shows: "92% recycler records verified · 84% price observations sourced · 12% synthetic." Every demo screen carries a DEMO vs REAL tag — say it before judges ask. Also build the local vocabulary layer ("board" → PCB → Printed Circuit Board, per language) — informal terminology ≠ formal taxonomy, a real design problem documented by the World Bank-backed 2,500-stakeholder Chennai survey.

MetalMandi = one company's funnel; ScrapUncle/TheKabadiwala = household-side, own payroll; Kabadiwalla Connect = city-scale general-MSW infrastructure. We are the neutral, offline-first, vernacular, e-waste-specific collector-side layer — cite all three accurately





Is the QR an EPR certificate?



No — it's a verifiable handover/chain-of-custody record; official EPR certificates remain in CPCB's registered-recycler workflow. Our schema is EPR-manifest-compatible so integration is a roadmap item, not a rewrite





What about batteries?



Routed via a separate regulatory track — Battery Waste Management Rules 2022 and CPCB's Battery EPR portal; we never treat battery lots as E-Waste-Rules activity





What if AI misclassifies?



AI suggests with confidence; human confirms; low confidence → manual selection; declared icon-picker fallback. AI never blocks the core transaction





Where are your training images from?



Published license/size/limitation table: Roboflow CC-BY sets + TrashBox/TrashNet + self-captured field photos; we report macro-F1 and limitations, not production-grade claims

WhatsApp has communication; it has no structured lots, source-aware prices, verified recycler matching, offline ledger, or standardized chain-of-custody in one workflow





Free tier goes down?

Add a new material live?



materials is admin-driven — add row, appears in app; we can do it on stage

What happens with recycler non-payment?

Where's your data from?



Provenance on every record: official registry vs field research vs synthetic, with sources and timestamps — we never present demo data as government data
